Understanding MacBook Accounts

To ensure a safe and managed learning environment, your child's MacBook is configured with specific user profiles. Here is how they work.

Important Note Regarding Logins

The IT Office will send an email with detailed login instructions after the MacBook distribution process is complete. Please look out for this email to complete the final setup steps at home.

Admin

Parent Account

This is the Administrator account. It allows you to manage the MacBook, install software, and set up Apple Screen Time / Parental Controls.

  • Keep this secure.
  • Do not give your child access to this password.

Student Account

This is a Standard User account. This is where your child will log in daily to complete schoolwork, access Managebac, and create projects.

  • Used for daily learning.
  • Monitored by school policies and parent screen time rules.

IT Support Account

This is a hidden administrative account used exclusively by the VSA IT Department. It allows our technicians to provide remote support, push necessary updates, and troubleshoot hardware issues at school.

Power Tips for Tech Success

1. Healthy Balanced Lifestyle

Nurture and support a whole-child approach. Ensure they have healthy meals, enough sleep, and ample physical/social exercise. Tech is just one part of their day.

2. Single Use for Learning

Make learning the one and only use for the VSA MacBook. Keep gaming, social media, and entertainment on separate home devices to build strong psychological boundaries.

3. Create a Study Zone

Set up a dedicated work/study zone at home. A place your child can organize and make their own, ideally in a shared family space where parent mentoring and passive monitoring can easily take place.

4. Set Clear Expectations

Have a "Media Talk." Co-create family tech rules. When and where are devices allowed? What are the "off" times? What happens if they encounter a problem online?

5. Tech-Free Dinners

Have regular family dinners with a strict "no phones/devices" rule. This is a critical time to talk, listen, and reconnect without digital distractions.

6. Sync Your Calendars

Import the Managebac Tasks Calendar and the VSA Parent Calendar into your personal phone calendar app. This lets you stay on top of deadlines without constantly logging in.

7. Focus on ATL (Approaches to Learning) Skills

Technology is a tool; the goal is building skills. Check in on how your child is learning. Ask them: How are you keeping your digital files organized? How do you track your to-do list? How do you manage your time when completing a task? Help them build these organizational systems early on.

Focus: Laptop Use in Year 6 Classes

This sections provides an insight for parents for how laptops are used within the classroom at Year 6 level. The below examples are drawn direct from subject teachers in term 1 of 2026-27 school year. These devices are key tools designed to enhance learning, build digital literacy, and foster essential Approaches to Learning (ATL) skills across subjects. Here is a look at how they are integrated.

Classroom Expectations & Guidance

"Screens Down"

Students immediately close or lower their laptop lids when the teacher is speaking or during non-digital instruction.

"Open Learning = Open Screen"

Active windows must be relevant to the class task. Background apps, hidden desktops, or tab-swapping are not permitted.

Teacher-Directed Usage

Laptops are used only when explicitly directed by the teacher for learning activities.

Assessment & Submissions

Specific assessment guidelines, rubrics, and templates will be posted and managed through ManageBac.

Subject-Specific Integration

Design

  • 3D Digital Design: Creating virtual worlds using tools like Delightex and TinkerCAD.
  • Documentation: Design ideas, process work, and learning reflections are documented using Google Slides.

Individuals & Societies

  • Structured Inquiries: Using research templates with guided questions to plan research.
  • Citing Sources: Learning to cite websites properly to support academic integrity.
  • Presentations: Building digital research presentations.

English Lang & Lit

  • Reading Comprehension: Utilising platforms like Read Theory for practice.
  • Collaborative Learning: Group collaboration via Google Drive, shared documents, and interactive platforms.

Science

  • Scientific Writing: Building research, lab report, and essay writing skills.

Language Acquisition

  • Vocab & Grammar: Interactive platforms (Quizlet, Kahoot, Blooket) reinforce structures.

Key Platforms

From Monitoring to Mentoring

As children transition into Secondary School, our role as parents must shift from strict authoritarian control to collaborative mentoring. We want to raise healthy adults, not just restrict children.

Avoid: Monitoring & Surveillance

  • Eroding trust through secret surveillance
  • Setting unilateral rules without discussion
  • "Helicopter" parenting
  • Doing the tech setup/organization for them
  • Relying only on tech systems (like Screen Time) to parent

Do: Coaching & Mentoring

  • Always protect and build mutual trust
  • Foster a collaborative, growth mindset
  • Scaffold support: Empower them when they are able, help when they aren't
  • Coach strategies for dealing with digital distractions
  • "Trust, but verify"

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about re-imaging, laptop care, and school digital policies.

What does "re-imaging" mean, and will it erase my child's files?

Re-imaging means replacing all existing contents of the hard disk with standardized VSA software and settings. This process will erase all content currently on the MacBook.

It is vital that students back up their important files before bringing their MacBook in for re-imaging. The school does not provide a backup service. We recommend students use their school Google Drive, which acts as a secure backup medium.

What happens to the software we (parents) already installed?

Any additional software you installed will be erased during re-imaging. You will need to re-install these programs once the laptop is returned to you. Parents will be provided with an Administrator account precisely so you can perform software installations and supervise the device at home.

Can students install games or other software on the laptops?

No. The student login is a standard user account with no authority to install software or modify essential computer settings. Only the Parent or IT Admin accounts have the rights to install software or perform system configurations.

What happens if the laptop is accidentally damaged?

If there is any damage, students should bring the laptop to the school’s IT Office for immediate inspection.

MacBooks purchased through the school vendor program (Senco) include AppleCare+, which extends coverage to three years and adds up to two incidents of accidental damage protection every 12 months (subject to a reduced service fee). These devices are also eligible for a loan unit during repair.

MacBooks not purchased through the school program will receive technical advice, but repair logistics remain the family's responsibility.

What happens if my laptop is lost or stolen?

As with other school property issued to students (such as library books), the family is responsible if the item is lost or stolen. The student will need to purchase a replacement laptop.

What about protective cases and carrying bags?

VSA does not provide a separate laptop carrying bag, as carrying a second bag alongside a regular school backpack is not ideal. Parents should ensure their child's school bag provides adequate room and protection.

We highly recommend purchasing a protective hard shell case or padded sleeve. Since all school MacBooks look identical, using a colored case, unique stickers (on the case, not the laptop), or distinct name tags is crucial for easy identification.

What is the expectation for battery life and charging?

Experience has shown that one full charge is generally enough for a whole day of school. Students are expected to arrive at school with their computer fully charged. However, if a laptop runs out of battery, there are power points available in all VSA classrooms and public areas.

Do parents need to purchase additional educational software?

No. VSA utilizes cloud-based educational platforms (like Google Workspace and Managebac) as our backbone. The school does not require parents to purchase additional software titles for use in the coming school year.
